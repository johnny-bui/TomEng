# Native Conversational English — TomEng V3

## Voice and register

- Natural neutral US conversational English, suitable for actual speech, not translation exercises.
- Keep the simple version front and center. Optional variants can show *casual*, *neutral*, *more polite* when they are genuinely different and plausible.
- Learn the full chunk first; introduce a replaceable slot only when the grammar and meaning still work across examples.
- Prefer `Can I get an iced latte?` to formulaic `I would like to request an iced latte beverage.`
- Prefer `Sorry, I didn't catch that.` to `I do not comprehend your preceding statement.`
- Prefer `Let me check and get back to you.` to `I will reconfirm and respond at the nearest possible time.`
- Prefer `Let's make the main button easier to find.` to `I'd like to reduce friction in the main call-to-action.`
- Prefer `Let's hold the release until the fix is ready.` to `Should we postpone the hotfix until the issue is fixed?`
- Avoid filler variants where only an unrelated noun changes and makes the meaning absurd.

## Life-first scenarios

Food & drink; restaurant tables; groceries; shopping & exchanges; payment & receipts; directions; rides; hotels; invitations; family check-ins; pharmacy symptoms; personal-device issues; appointments; opinions; deliveries; urgent help; everyday small talk.

## How to compose a useful phrase card

Example: `Could I get an iced latte, please?`  
VN: `Cho mình một ly latte đá nhé?`  
When: order at a café  
Chunk: `Could I get + [item], please?`  
Cue: `Can I get…?` is the fastest polite phrase at a counter.  
Native variations: `I'll have an iced latte.` / `Can I get that to go?`  
Follow-up by **learner**: `That's all, thanks.`

Example: `Sorry, I missed the last part.`  
VN: `Xin lỗi, tôi không nghe kịp đoạn cuối.`  
Chunk: `I missed + [the detail]`  
Follow-up: `Could you say that again?`

## Critical distinction

"Native-style wording" refers to text idiomaticity; **not** a native human voice. Browser speech synthesis is TTS; microphone recognition is not a validated pronunciation grader. Do not advertise human review or recording that did not occur.

## Quality status

This dataset was compiled from authored phrase frames, edited for obvious mismatched slots, then sampled and validated with automated checks. It is an **AI editorial draft, not a guarantee of human native-speaker QA of 1,200 individual lines**. Codex must perform the manual-per-pattern/per-variant editorial pass before merge and report unresolved issues honestly. For a gold-standard product, a qualified native-speaking editor should review the final dataset.
